# Dutch income tax: one employer and a side income

## What it is

Inkomstenbelasting is the yearly income tax. The rules are in the Wet inkomstenbelasting 2001 (BWBR0011353);
the Belastingdienst explains them on its pages. This skill is for someone who lives in the Netherlands all
year, has one employer and earns something on the side. The figures are for **2026** and for someone who has
not reached AOW age. They change on 1 January, so check again before relying on one.

VAT on a side business is a different tax: see `dutch-vat-zzp`. Starting a real business: see
`starting-as-zzp`. Allowances that depend on this income: see `toeslagen`.

## Box 1 in a few lines

Income is taxed in boxes. **Box 1** is income from work and home: wages, profit from a business, income from
other work (overig werk), benefits and pensions, and the home ownership items. From that come the box 1
deductions, such as the costs of the own home and the persoonsgebonden aftrek. **Box 3** is savings and
investments.

Box 1 rates for 2026, below AOW age (the first bracket includes the national insurance premiums):

| Taxable income box 1 | Rate |
|---|---|
| up to € 38,883 | 35.75% |
| € 38,883 to € 78,426 | 37.56% |
| above € 78,426 | 49.50% |

Deductions such as the persoonsgebonden aftrek, the own home costs and the ondernemersaftrek do not save 49.50%
in the top bracket. The law caps their effect there at the second bracket rate (art. 2.10, second and third
paragraphs). Tell the owner this before they count on a deduction.

A loss in box 1 can be set off against box 1 income of the 3 previous and 9 following years.

**Box 3 in 2026**: the tax-free amount (heffingsvrij vermogen) is € 59,357, or € 118,714 with a fiscal
partner. The Belastingdienst calls the 2026 return percentages provisional, so do not calculate box 3 tax for
the owner from them; point to the official calculation.

## The side income: which kind is it?

What the side income is decides what may be deducted.

- **Winst uit onderneming**: the owner is an entrepreneur for income tax. The Belastingdienst judges this on
  facts such as profit, independence, capital, time, several clients, being known to the outside world and
  business risk. Only then do the ondernemersaftrek and similar schemes apply. See `starting-as-zzp`.
- **Inkomsten uit overig werk**: not employed and not an entrepreneur, but paid for work. Examples from the
  Belastingdienst: guest lectures, delivering post or flyers, income from apps or online selling, tutoring,
  medical trials, being paid from someone's pgb. The profit is calculated the same way as for entrepreneurs
  and some costs may be deducted, but there is **no zelfstandigenaftrek and no investeringsaftrek**.
- **Hobby or family sphere**: a job for family or friends where only costs are paid back, or activities that
  keep making a loss, are not reported.

With income from overig werk, the owner also pays an **income-related Zvw contribution**, in a separate
assessment after the return. Warn them so the second letter is not a surprise. There is no legal duty to keep
books for overig werk, but the Belastingdienst can ask for the figures, so keep the receipts.

In practice no employer withholds tax on a side income, so the tax on it arrives after the return. The
voorlopige aanslag (below) spreads it over the year.

## Tax credits (heffingskortingen) at a high level

Tax credits are subtracted from the tax, not from income. The main ones for 2026, below AOW age:

- **Algemene heffingskorting**: € 3,115 for everyone, reduced by 6.398% of the verzamelinkomen above
  € 29,736, and € 0 from € 78,426 (art. 8.10).
- **Arbeidskorting**: for income from work. It rises with work income to € 5,685 at € 45,592, then falls,
  and is € 0 from € 132,920.
- **Inkomensafhankelijke combinatiekorting**: a credit for some working parents, up to € 3,032. Check the
  conditions on the Belastingdienst page before saying the owner gets it.

The Belastingdienst applies the rates in the return itself; the owner does not calculate them by hand.

## The return and its deadline

- **Got an aangiftebrief?** Then the owner must file before the date in the letter. Usually that is **1 May**
  of the year after the tax year.
- **No letter?** For the 2025 return the rule was: fill it in without sending. If it shows **€ 58 or more to
  pay**, file as soon as possible and before 14 July, or risk a fine. If it shows **€ 19 or more back**, file
  to get it. With toeslagen and a vermogen above € 37,395 (€ 74,790 with a fiscal partner), file anyway.
  These amounts are for the 2025 return; check them again for a later year.
- **More time**: the owner can ask for uitstel. With uitstel the return is usually due
  on 1 September. Filing late brings belastingrente (tax interest).

What happens when it is late: a reminder (herinnering), then a demand (aanmaning) with 10 working days to
file. After that a **verzuimboete of € 469**, higher for repeat cases (up to € 6,709). The Belastingdienst
can also stop paying out toeslagen and the voorlopige aanslag. Without any return it estimates the income and
sends an assessment plus a fine. A deliberately wrong return can bring a vergrijpboete.

## The voorlopige aanslag

A voorlopige aanslag is an estimate for the current year, paid or received per month. It is based on what
the Belastingdienst knows, such as last year's return. It is settled against the final assessment.

Change it in Mijn Belastingdienst when, for example, the owner:

- starts earning more or less, including a new side income;
- buys a house, or the mortgage interest changes;
- gives more or less to charities, or gets more or fewer deductions such as zorgkosten;
- marries, starts living together, separates, or stops a business.

Changing it is not required, but it prevents a large bill later. It can usually be requested or changed until
**1 May of the next year**, or until the filing deadline in the letter or the uitstel date. Once the return
for that year is filed, it cannot be changed any more.

## Deductions a person might miss

These are persoonsgebonden aftrekposten (art. 6.1) and other box 1 items from the Belastingdienst's own
lists:

- **Giften (gifts to charity)**: only to an institution registered as an ANBI (check it with the ANBI
  search on the Belastingdienst site). The gift must be provable, for example by bank statement, not in cash,
  and with nothing received in return. Lottery tickets do not count. Ordinary gifts only count above a
  threshold of **1% of the drempelinkomen, at least € 60**, and up to 10% of it. **Periodieke giften**, fixed
  in a deed for at least 5 years, have no threshold.
- **Specifieke zorgkosten**: some costs of illness or disability for the owner, a fiscal partner or children
  under 27, such as prescribed medicines, certain aids, transport to a doctor or hospital, and some treatments.
  Costs under the eigen risico, or that insurance reimburses or could reimburse, do not count. Only the part
  above an income-dependent threshold is deductible.
- **Reisaftrek openbaar vervoer**, **lijfrente premiums** (uitgaven voor inkomensvoorzieningen) and **paid
  partner alimony** are other box 1 deductions.
- **Costs of the side income**, when it is overig werk or a business.

Studiekosten can no longer be deducted: that ended after tax year 2021.

## The 30% ruling, only at a high level

The expatregeling (30%-regeling) lets an **employer** pay a tax-free allowance for extraterritorial costs of
at most 30% of the wage including the allowance. Conditions: the employee is employed by that employer, has a
specific expertise (in 2026 this means, among other things, a wage above € 48,013 excluding the
allowance), counts as an incoming employee, and the Belastingdienst has issued a **beschikking**. The maximum
tax-free allowance in 2026 is € 78,600. It runs through the employer's payroll: the owner checks it on the
payslip and asks HR. For anything more, point to a tax adviser.

## What to check before filing

1. The letter: is there an aangiftebrief, and what date is in it?
2. The prefilled data (salary, bank accounts and the like) against the owner's own jaaropgaven. Prefilled is
   not the same as correct.
3. The side income: which kind is it, what came in, what costs, and the receipts.
4. Paid and received voorlopige aanslag amounts for that year.
5. Deductions: gifts with proof and ANBI status, care costs with invoices and insurer statements.
6. A fiscal partner: some items can be divided between partners. Look at both returns together.
7. Toeslagen: the income in the return becomes the income for the toeslagen of that year. See `toeslagen`.

## How to work with it

1. Establish the facts first: employer, the side income and its kind, partner, house, savings, toeslagen.
2. Give dates worked out: "Je aangifte 2026 moet binnen zijn vóór de datum in je aangiftebrief, meestal
   1 mei 2027."
3. When a side income starts, suggest changing the voorlopige aanslag, with the reason.
4. For a rule or a figure, name the Belastingdienst page or the article; read it with the `dutch-laws`
   server (BWBR0011353) when the exact text matters.
5. Filing, changing a voorlopige aanslag or requesting uitstel is done in the owner's name. Prepare it, show
   it, and only submit on an explicit yes (see `acting-on-behalf`).

## What not to claim

- Do not say a side income is "a business" or "overig werk" as a fact. The Belastingdienst decides.
- Do not promise a refund or name an amount the owner will pay; the assessment decides.
- Do not give figures from memory, or 2026 figures for another year.
- Do not claim the owner has the 30% ruling or a tax credit without the payslip or the conditions.

## Where this stops

This follows the Wet inkomstenbelasting 2001 as in force in 2026 and the Belastingdienst's pages on box 1,
the 2026 rates and tax credits, filing and deadlines, uitstel, the voorlopige aanslag, income from other work,
gifts, care costs and the expatregeling. It does not cover box 2, emigration or immigration during the year,
foreign income, a bv, inheritance or gift tax, or a tax audit. For those, or when a lot of money depends on
it, point to the BelastingTelefoon or a tax adviser.
