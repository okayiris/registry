# The EU AI Act, the basics

## What it is

The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is the EU's law on artificial intelligence. It sets rules by risk:
most AI use has no rules at all, some uses need transparency, some are high-risk with strict duties, and a
few are banned. It was amended in 2026 by the Digital Omnibus on AI, which moved several dates. This skill
follows the Commission's own current summary and timeline.

## Does it apply to the owner?

- **Purely personal, non-professional use is outside it.** The Act does not apply to the obligations of
  people using AI systems in a purely personal non-professional activity (Article 2(10)). An owner using
  their assistant for their own household is not a regulated "deployer".
- **Work and business use is inside it.** An owner who uses AI in their business, for example a chatbot on
  their site, AI-written posts for their company, or AI to screen applicants, is a deployer with duties.
- Those who build or sell AI systems (providers) have the heaviest duties; this skill is about deployers.

## The four levels of risk

| Level | What it means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Unacceptable** | Banned | See the list below |
| **High risk** | Strict duties before use: risk management, data quality, logging, documentation, human oversight | CV-sorting for recruitment, credit scoring, exam scoring, AI in critical infrastructure |
| **Transparency** | People must be told | Chatbots, deep fakes, AI text informing the public |
| **Minimal or none** | No rules | Spam filters, AI in video games; most AI in use today |

## The banned practices

Nine practices are prohibited:

1. harmful AI-based manipulation and deception;
2. harmful exploitation of vulnerabilities;
3. social scoring;
4. assessing or predicting the risk that a person commits a crime;
5. untargeted scraping of the internet or CCTV to build facial recognition databases;
6. emotion recognition in workplaces and education;
7. biometric categorisation to deduce certain protected characteristics;
8. real-time remote biometric identification for law enforcement in public spaces;
9. AI that generates non-consensual sexually explicit or intimate content, or child sexual abuse material,
   such as "nudification" apps.

Bans 1 to 8 apply since **February 2025**; ban 9 was added by the 2026 amendments and applies from
**2 December 2026**.

## Transparency (Article 50), since 2 August 2026

- **A chatbot or assistant that talks with people** must let them know they are dealing with an AI, unless
  that is obvious.
- **Deep fakes** (images, audio or video that look real) must be disclosed as generated or manipulated.
- **AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest** must be disclosed,
  unless a person reviewed it and someone holds editorial responsibility.
- The information must be clear and given at the latest at the first interaction or exposure.

For how an assistant keeps these rules when it writes or calls on the owner's behalf, see `acting-on-behalf`.

## AI literacy (Article 4)

Businesses that use AI take measures to support the AI literacy of their staff and others who operate AI
for them: enough understanding to use it with awareness of its opportunities, risks and possible harm. It
does not require a specific level or a certificate.

## Key dates

| Date | What applies |
|---|---|
| 1 August 2024 | The Act enters into force |
| 2 February 2025 | Definitions, AI literacy, bans 1 to 8 |
| 2 August 2025 | Rules for general-purpose AI models; national authorities in place |
| **2 August 2026** | Most rules, including transparency (Article 50); enforcement starts |
| 2 December 2026 | Ban 9; transition deadline for some generated-content providers |
| 2 December 2027 | High-risk rules for Annex III uses (such as recruitment, credit) |
| 2 August 2028 | High-risk rules for AI in regulated products (Annex I) |

## How the assistant helps

1. Ask what the AI is used for, and whether it reaches other people.
2. Place it in a risk level. Most uses are minimal; say so, so the owner does not over-worry.
3. For a chatbot, a public AI text or generated images: name the transparency duty and help word the
   notice.
4. For anything that looks high-risk (hiring, credit, education, access to services) or near a ban: say so
   plainly, and point to the AI Act Service Desk or a lawyer before the owner goes ahead.

## What not to claim

- The Act was amended in 2026 and guidance keeps appearing. Check the Commission's timeline before stating a
  date.
- Do not classify a borderline system as definitely high-risk or definitely not; point to the guidance.
- This is not legal advice.
