# GDPR basics

## What it is

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, in Dutch AVG) protects personal data in the whole EU. It
applies to any organisation handling the personal data of people in the EU, also organisations based
outside the EU that offer goods or services here. It does **not** apply to a person's purely private use,
such as their own address book (see `private-assistant-discretion` for how an assistant handles that).

This skill has two halves: the owner **as a person** with rights, and the owner **as a business** with
duties.

## The owner's rights

| Right | What the owner can ask | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Access** | Which data an organisation has about them, a free copy, and how it is used | Answer due within 1 month |
| **Correction** | Correct wrong or incomplete data | |
| **Erasure** ("right to be forgotten") | Delete data that is no longer needed or used unlawfully; also links in search engines that are inaccurate, irrelevant or excessive | Not absolute: data kept by legal obligation or for freedom of expression may stay |
| **Portability** | Get their data back, or sent to another service, where technically possible | |
| **Objecting** | Stop use based on the organisation's legitimate interest, and **always** stop direct marketing | Marketing must stop at once |
| **Withdrawing consent** | Withdraw consent given earlier, at any time | |
| **Complaint** | Complain to the national data protection authority, which responds within 3 months, or go to court | Compensation is possible for material or non-material damage |

Requests to organisations are free of charge, and the organisation may ask for proof of identity.

### How the assistant helps with a request

1. Find the organisation's contact for data protection (often a privacy page or a Data Protection Officer).
2. Draft a short request: who the owner is, which right they use, what exactly they ask, and that an answer
   is due within one month. The owner approves and sends it (see `acting-on-behalf`).
3. Note the date sent and the date an answer is due; check back then (see `personal-triage`).
4. No answer or a refusal without good reason: help draft a complaint to the national data protection
   authority.

### A data breach

When an organisation's data about the owner was stolen, lost or accessed illegally, it must report this to
the authority, and tell the owner directly if there are serious risks. Help the owner act: change the
password there and anywhere it was reused, and watch for fake messages that use the leaked details (see
`personal-triage`).

## The owner as a business

Once the owner handles other people's data for work (clients, customers, members, a mailing list), the
GDPR applies to that part. The basics:

- **A lawful reason for each use:** a contract (to deliver what was bought), a legal obligation (records for
  the tax office), vital interests, a public task, legitimate interests, or otherwise **consent**. Consent
  must be a clear opt-in; a pre-ticked box or an opt-out is not consent.
- **The seven principles:** lawful, fair and transparent; only for the stated purpose; only the data needed;
  accurate; kept no longer than needed; secure; and able to show all this.
- **Tell people** who you are, why you use their data, how long you keep it, who else gets it, and what
  their rights are.
- **Marketing mail** needs prior consent. To existing customers, mail about similar own products is allowed,
  with an easy way to object.
- **Sensitive data** (such as health, religion, political opinions, ethnic origin, sex life or sexual
  orientation, genetic and biometric data) may only be processed under specific conditions. Avoid it unless
  the work truly needs it.
- **Answer requests** from people within one month, free of charge.
- **Cookies** on the owner's website that are not strictly necessary need consent first.

## What not to claim

- National law adds details (for the Netherlands, for example, the rule on the BSN in the UAVG); check the
  country.
- For a business with many people's data, sensitive data or monitoring, point to a privacy adviser.
- This is not legal advice.

## Where this stops

This follows the EU's official guidance on Your Europe for data protection and the European Commission's
explanation of the GDPR for organisations.
