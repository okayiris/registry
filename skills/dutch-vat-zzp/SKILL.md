# Dutch VAT for a zzp'er

## What it is

Btw (omzetbelasting) is charged on what a business sells and passed on to the Belastingdienst. What the
business paid in VAT on its own costs (voorbelasting) is deducted. The rules are in the Wet op de
omzetbelasting 1968; the Belastingdienst explains them on its pages. Both are named at the end.

This skill is for a sole trader or zzp'er established in the Netherlands, selling mostly to Dutch
customers and sometimes to businesses in other EU countries. The figures are as of **2026**. Check a
figure again before you rely on it: they change on 1 January.

## Two numbers, never mix them up

A zzp'er registered for VAT gets two numbers, each in its own letter:

- **btw-identificatienummer (btw-id)**, like `NL123456789B01`: for customers and suppliers. It goes on
  invoices, the website and letterheads.
- **omzetbelastingnummer (ob-nummer)**: only for contact with the Belastingdienst. For a zzp'er it contains
  their BSN. **Never put it on an invoice or a website.** Doing so publishes the owner's BSN.

## Rates

- **21%**: the general rate, for everything that is not exempt and not 9% or 0%.
- **9%**: the reduced rate, among others for food and some services. The exact list is Tabel I of the
  law.
- **0%**: mainly exports and goods or services to other countries. No VAT is charged, but VAT on costs may
  still be deducted.
- **Exempt (vrijgesteld)**: no VAT is charged, and VAT on costs may **not** be deducted. That is the
  difference from 0%.

When unsure which rate applies, say so and point to the Belastingdienst page. Do not guess a rate.

## The small business scheme (KOR)

The KOR is a VAT exemption for businesses established in the Netherlands with a turnover of **at most
€20,000 a calendar year**. The limit holds for the year of joining and for the year before (art. 25a Wet
OB).

Taking part means:

- no VAT on invoices, and no invoices needed for VAT at all. If the owner does send one, it must say that
  an exemption applies;
- no VAT returns, except incidental ones (for example after buying a new car in another EU country);
- **no deduction of VAT on costs and investments**, including VAT paid abroad. Purchase invoices must still
  be kept;
- leaving is possible at any time and takes effect on the first day of the next quarter;
- **the moment turnover passes €20,000 in a calendar year, the owner must leave at once**, and VAT applies
  from that supply on.

What counts as turnover: supplies at 21%, 9% and 0%, domestic reverse-charge supplies, and some exempt
turnover (property, financial and insurance services). What does not count: supplies taxed in another
country, private use, the sale of the business's own investment goods, and purchases from other EU
countries. All subnumbers of one entrepreneur count together, and the choice applies to all of them.

Joining is done online in Mijn Belastingdienst Zakelijk. It does not start at once, so check the current
start date and deadline on the Belastingdienst's own KOR pages before promising a date. Until the KOR has
started, the owner keeps filing returns.

**Weighing the KOR** follows from the rules above. Private customers cannot deduct VAT, so to them a price
without VAT is simply lower. Business customers can deduct it, so to them it makes no difference. What the
owner gives up is the deduction of VAT on their own costs, which weighs most in a year with investments. Lay
out both sides with the owner's own numbers, and let them decide.

## Invoices

A zzp'er must invoice every business customer, and send the invoice by the 15th of the month after the
month of supply. A compliant invoice shows (art. 35a Wet OB):

- full name and address of both parties, the actual address and not only a PO box;
- the owner's **btw-id** (not the ob-nummer), and the KVK number when registered there;
- the invoice date and a unique number from one or more consecutive series;
- what was supplied: quantity and kind of goods, or kind and extent of the service;
- the date of supply (or of an advance payment), when different from the invoice date;
- the amount excluding VAT per rate, the unit price where relevant, the rate and the VAT amount.

Lighter rules apply to invoices of €100 or less including VAT; extra ones apply to foreign customers.

## Filing and paying

Most businesses file **per quarter**. The return **and the payment** must be **received** by the last day
of the month after the period. For 2026:

| Period | Must be received by |
|---|---|
| Q4 2025 | 31 January 2026 |
| Q1 2026 | 30 April 2026 |
| Q2 2026 | 31 July 2026 |
| Q3 2026 | 31 October 2026 |
| Q4 2026 | 31 January 2027 |

Monthly returns follow the same rule per month. An annual return, which is only possible on request,
must be in before **1 April** of the next year.

- A period without any VAT still needs a return (a nihil-aangifte).
- Late filing or payment brings an additional assessment with an estimated amount, and fines.

## Reverse charge (btw verlegd)

With reverse charge, the customer pays the VAT instead of the supplier. The supplier's invoice then shows:

- no VAT, but the words **"btw verlegd"**;
- the customer's **btw-id**;
- the amount per rate as it would have been.

The supplier stays responsible. If it later turns out the reverse charge did not apply, or the customer
cannot be identified, the Belastingdienst can collect the VAT from the supplier. Check before applying it.

**Services to a business in another EU country** are usually taxed in the customer's country. Check the
customer's VAT number first, then:

1. invoice without VAT, marked "btw verlegd", with the customer's btw-id;
2. report it in the return under **3b**;
3. also report it in the **opgaaf ICP** (intracommunity supplies).

A customer without a VAT number, like a private person elsewhere in the EU, usually gets Dutch VAT for a
service. Some services are exceptions.

## A mistake in a filed return

- **At most €1,000** too much or too little: correct it in the next return, under the heading where it
  belongs.
- **More than €1,000**: file a correction (suppletie btw) in Mijn Belastingdienst Zakelijk as soon as
  possible, and in any case **within 8 weeks** of finding the mistake. Otherwise a fine is possible.
- Corrections are possible up to five years after the year concerned.
- Too little VAT reported: wait for the additional assessment before paying.

## Keeping records

Records, including all invoices sent and received, are kept for **7 years**. Records about real estate
are kept for 10 years.

## How to work with it

1. Establish first whether the owner is registered for VAT, takes part in the KOR, and files quarterly.
   Everything else follows from that.
2. Reading their bookkeeping (Moneybird, Stripe)? Check invoices against the list above, and check that
   the btw-id, not the ob-nummer, is on them.
3. Remind them of a deadline with the date from the table, and say that it is the date the payment must be
   **received**, not sent.
4. For a rate, a foreign customer or the KOR choice, give the rule and the page it comes from.
5. With the `dutch-laws` server, read the article itself for the date that matters: art. 9 (rates),
   25a (KOR), 35a (invoice) and 12 (reverse charge) of BWBR0002629.

## What not to claim

- Do not decide the KOR for the owner, and do not file anything in their name without an explicit yes.
- Do not name a rate for a specific product or service without the source that says so.
- Do not give numbers from memory for a year other than 2026.
- Do not treat this as advice for a bv, a foreign business, margin or travel agency schemes, property, or
  e-commerce to consumers across the EU (OSS). Those have their own rules. Say so and refer the owner to a
  tax adviser or the BelastingTelefoon.
