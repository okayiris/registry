# Huurrecht for tenants

## What it is

The rules between a tenant and a landlord of a home (woonruimte) in the Netherlands. They are in the
Burgerlijk Wetboek Boek 7 and related laws; Rijksoverheid, Volkshuisvesting Nederland and the Huurcommissie
explain them. This skill is for a **tenant** of a home and uses the figures for **2026**. Figures change on
1 January and 1 July; check again before relying on one.

First establish three facts. Almost every answer depends on them:

1. **Which sector** the home is in: social, midden or free (below).
2. **When the contract was signed**: before or on/after 1 July 2024.
3. **What kind of contract**: indefinite (vast) or temporary (tijdelijk).

## The three sectors (Wet betaalbare huur)

Since 1 July 2024 the points system (woningwaarderingsstelsel, WWS) decides the maximum rent for social and
middle-rent homes. It was modernised: WOZ value, outdoor space and the energy label count differently, and a
poor energy label gives minus points. Free-sector homes have no maximum rent.

The sector depends on the points **and** the date the contract was signed (not when it started):

| Sector | Contract signed before 1 July 2024 | Contract signed on/after 1 July 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Social | up to 143 points (starting rent at or below the liberalisation limit of that time) | up to 143 points |
| Midden | does not exist for these contracts | 144 to 186 points |
| Free | starting rent above the liberalisation limit of that time | 187 points or more |

For contracts starting on or after 1 January 2026: the upper limit of social rent (143 points) is
**€932.93**, and the upper limit of middle rent (186 points) is **€1,228.07** a month. Rooms, caravans and
pitches always fall under social rent. Since 1 January 2025 a landlord must add a points count to every new
contract. The Huurcommissie's **Huurprijscheck** calculates the points.

Special case: a free-sector contract from before 1 July 2024 for a home that has only up to 143 points. Since
1 July 2025 that tenant can ask the Huurcommissie to lower the rent on points, after first proposing a lower
rent to the landlord.

## Rent too high from the start

- **Social or midden, or a room:** within **6 months** after the contract started, the tenant can ask the
  Huurcommissie to assess the starting rent (toetsing aanvangshuurprijs). If it is too high, the lower rent
  applies from the start of the contract. Keep paying the old rent until the ruling; afterwards ask the
  landlord to pay back the difference. A lower rent can mean less huurtoeslag.
- Living there longer than 6 months in social or middle rent: propose a lower rent to the landlord first; if
  they refuse, go to the Huurcommissie.
- **Free sector:** the Huurcommissie can assess the starting rent within the first 6 months, or with a
  temporary contract of at most 2 years. After that it cannot rule on a free-sector rent, unless the home
  really belongs in the social sector by points.

## Rent increases in 2026

A landlord may raise the rent **once in 12 months**, usually on 1 July. Exceptions: the first year of the
contract, a late increase moved to a later date, and an increase after the landlord improved the home
(separate from the yearly increase).

| Sector | Maximum yearly increase 2026 |
|---|---|
| Social, rent €350 or more | **4.1%** from 1 July 2026 (until then 5%) |
| Social, rent under €350 | **€25** a month |
| Social, higher middle income (zelfstandige woning) | **€50** a month |
| Social, high income (zelfstandige woning) | **€100** a month |
| Room, caravan, pitch | 4.1%, never income-dependent |
| Midden | **6.1%** from 1 January 2026 |
| Free sector | **4.4%** (1 January 2026 to 1 January 2027) |

Income limits for the social increase use the household's 2024 income:

| Household | 4.1% / €25 | €50 | €100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 person | €59,504 or less | €59,504 to €70,149 | €70,149 or more |
| 2 or more | €68,858 or less | €68,858 to €93,531 | €93,531 or more |

For children living at home and under 23 on 1 January 2026, only their income above €26,819 counts.

These are maximums. A lower increase in the contract applies; a higher one in the contract does not.

### Social sector: the proposal and objecting

- The landlord sends a written proposal (letter or e-mail) at least **2 months** before the date. It states
  the old and new basic rent, the increase, the date, and how to object. For an income-based increase, the
  Belastingdienst's income statement comes with it.
- To object, the tenant writes to the **landlord**, **before the date** of the increase. The Huurcommissie
  has a model letter (bezwaarschrift huurverhoging 2026). Grounds include a temporary rent reduction for
  defects, a rent above the points maximum, or errors in the proposal. With the standard 4.1% or €25 increase,
  income is not a ground; with an income-based increase it can be (for example lower income in 2025).
- If the landlord keeps the increase, **the landlord** must ask the Huurcommissie within 6 weeks after the
  date. The tenant cannot take an objection to the Huurcommissie themselves.
- Paying the new rent shows agreement (art. 7:253 BW). If the tenant neither objects nor pays, the landlord
  may send a registered reminder within 3 months; the tenant then has 4 months after the date to go to the
  Huurcommissie, or is deemed to agree.
- Defects alone are no ground to refuse the increase unless a defects procedure (gebrekenprocedure) has
  already been started.

### Midden and free sector

- The increase follows the contract; there is no fixed announcement moment in the free sector.
- Above the maximum is never allowed, even if the contract says more. Until 1 May 2029 a free-sector landlord
  may not offer a new contract with a higher basic rent for the same home, nor end the tenancy because the
  tenant refuses a higher rent.
- Free sector: over 4.4% and no agreement with the landlord? The tenant can go to the Huurcommissie up to
  4 months after the date of the increase.
- Midden and free sector: here only the tenant can file a request with the Huurcommissie.

## Fixed and temporary contracts (Wet vaste huurcontracten)

Since 1 July 2024 a new tenant gets an **indefinite** contract. A temporary contract of at most **2 years**
is only allowed for specific groups: a student renting in another municipality for study, someone living
elsewhere during work or renovation on their own home, someone leaving shelter or in a social emergency, a
second-chance contract, a surviving household member who does not continue as co-tenant, a divorcing parent
renting near their children, someone working on a Wadden island, and a status holder coming from a COA
location. Tussenhuur (the diplomatenclausule) also remains possible.

For such a temporary contract:

- the landlord must inform the tenant in writing **no earlier than 3 months and no later than 1 month**
  before the end date; if not, it continues for an indefinite period;
- the tenant may end it early;
- a renewal, or a new contract straight after with the same tenant, is indefinite.

Temporary contracts signed before 1 July 2024 run to their end date unchanged; renewed, they become indefinite.

An indefinite contract can only be ended by the landlord on legal grounds, stated in the notice, by
registered letter or bailiff. Notice periods: tenant one rent period, at least 1 and at most 3 months;
landlord at least 3 months plus 1 month per year of tenancy, up to 6.

## Service costs

- Service costs and costs for utilities with an individual meter are what tenant and landlord agreed.
- The landlord gives an itemised yearly overview with the method of calculation, **at the latest six months
  after the calendar year**. On request the tenant may see the underlying books and documents.
- If they do not agree, either side can ask the Huurcommissie, for one period of at most 12 months per cost
  type, up to **24 months** after the deadline for the overview (art. 7:259 and 7:260 BW).
- The Huurcommissie rules on this for all contracts signed on or after 1 July 2024, and for social-sector
  contracts from before. For an older free-sector contract it can only advise, if both sides agreed in
  writing.

## Deposit (waarborgsom)

- At most **2 months' basic rent** for contracts from 1 July 2023 (3 months before that).
- Returned within **14 days** after the tenancy ends; within **30 days** if the landlord deducts something.
- Only four things may be deducted: rent arrears, service costs, damage the tenant is responsible for, and
  the energy performance fee. Not administration fees, not normal wear. The landlord must send a full written
  specification. No interest is owed.
- Too high a deposit: refuse (and probably lose the home), pay and report it to the municipality, or pay and
  ask the court to lower it.
- Get proof of payment; be present at the handover inspection, or take photos.

## Defects and repairs

- The landlord must repair defects on request, except small repairs that are the tenant's own, and damage
  the tenant caused (art. 7:206 BW). The tenant can claim a rent reduction from the day they properly reported
  the defect (art. 7:207 BW).
- Report in writing: the Huurcommissie has a model letter, or use e-mail with the landlord's address and date
  visible. Give the landlord **6 weeks**.
- No fix within 6 weeks and a **serious** defect (categories A, B, C: for example no running water, serious
  mould, rain leaks)? Start a case at the Huurcommissie. Started within 6 months of the report, the reduction
  usually starts on the first of the month after the report.
- The Huurcommissie does not rule on compensation or damage to belongings; that is insurance or the
  kantonrechter.
- The law lets a tenant repair and deduct reasonable costs when the landlord is in default (art. 7:206 lid 3
  BW). Whether the landlord is in default is a legal question; get advice before deducting anything.

## Going to the Huurcommissie

1. Log in at **MijnHuurcommissie** with DigiD, choose "Start een nieuwe procedure" and the right form.
2. Add documents: contract, letters, proof of sending. Help by phone: 088 - 1 600 300.
3. Pay the advance fee: **€25** for a tenant (a landlord pays €500). Not paid, or late: the case stops.
   Won: the fee comes back. With a minimum income or benefit level, ask for exemption within 4 weeks of the
   payment request.

## How to work with it

1. Read the landlord's letter with the owner. Find sector, contract date and contract type.
2. Check the increase against the table and the proposal against the list of required items.
3. Put the deadline in the agenda: the date of the increase for an objection, 6 months for the starting rent,
   6 weeks after a defects letter.
4. Draft the letter from the Huurcommissie model; the owner signs and sends it (`acting-on-behalf`). Keep
   copies and proof of sending.

## What not to claim

- Do not calculate the points yourself as if final. Use the Huurprijscheck and call the result an estimate.
- Do not advise withholding or deducting rent on your own reading; that step is for Het Juridisch Loket or
  a lawyer.
- Do not promise the Huurcommissie will agree; do not guess figures for 2027 or other years.

## Where this stops
